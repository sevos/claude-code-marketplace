## Phonology and Pronunciation

Sindarin was designed by Tolkien with a Welsh-like phonology, giving it a distinctly melodic and flowing sound. Understanding proper pronunciation is essential for speaking Sindarin correctly.

### Vowels

Sindarin has six monophthongal vowels, unlike Quenya which has five:

| Letter | Sound | IPA | Example |
|--------|-------|-----|---------|
| a | as in "father" | [a] | *adan* (man) |
| e | as in "bet" | [ɛ] | *edhel* (elf) |
| i | as in "machine" | [i] | *Imladris* (Rivendell) |
| o | as in "for" | [ɔ] | *orch* (orc) |
| u | as in "brute" | [u] | *dulu* (support) |
| y | as in French "lune" or German "u-umlaut" | [y] | *ylf* (drinking vessel) |

**Note:** The vowel **y** is unique to Sindarin among Tolkien's Elvish languages. It sounds like the French pronunciation of "u" in "lune" or the German "u-umlaut."

### Long Vowels

Vowels can be lengthened, indicated by an acute accent or a circumflex:

| Short | Long | Sound |
|-------|------|-------|
| a | a/a | [aː] |
| e | e/e | [eː] |
| i | i/i | [iː] |
| o | o/o | [ɔː] |
| u | u/u | [uː] |

### Diphthongs

Tolkien listed six primary diphthongs in *The Lord of the Rings* Appendix E. All are "falling" diphthongs, stressed on the first element:

| Diphthong | Pronunciation | Example |
|-----------|---------------|---------|
| ai | as in "rye" | *edain* (men) |
| ei | as in "grey" | *teithant* (drew) |
| ui | as in "ruin" | *Fanuilos* (Ever-white) |
| au (aw) | as in "loud" or "how" | *naug* (dwarf) |
| ae | "ah-eh" run together | *aear* (sea) |
| oe | "oh-eh" run together | *noeg* (dwarves) |

### Consonants

Most consonants are pronounced as in English, with the following important exceptions:

| Letter | Sound | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| c | always hard [k] | Never soft; *Celeborn* = "Keleborn" |
| ch | as in Scottish "loch" or German "Bach" | [x] Never as in English "church" |
| dh | as in "that" or "the" | [ð] Voiced dental fricative |
| th | as in "thin" | [θ] Voiceless; never as in "that" |
| g | always hard | As in "get," never as in "gem" |
| ph | as in "phone" | [f] |
| rh | voiceless r | Breathy, aspirated r |
| lh | voiceless l | Breathy, aspirated l |
| hw | as in "white" (older pronunciation) | [ʍ] Voiceless w |

### Stress Rules

Stress in Sindarin follows a Latin-like pattern:

1. **Two-syllable words:** Stress falls on the first syllable
2. **Three or more syllables:**
   - Stress the **penult** (second-to-last syllable) if it is "heavy" (contains a long vowel, diphthong, or is closed by a consonant)
   - Otherwise, stress the **antepenult** (third-to-last syllable)

Examples:
- *elbereth* - stress on "el" (penult is light)
- *Imladris* - stress on "lad" (closed syllable)
- *Periannath* - stress on "an" (closed syllable)

## The Definite Article

Sindarin has a definite article "the" but no indefinite article ("a/an"). An indefinite noun is simply the bare noun.

### Forms of the Article

| Number | Article | Mutation Triggered | Example |
|--------|---------|-------------------|---------|
| Singular | i | Soft (Lenition) | *i vellon* "the friend" (from *mellon*) |
| Plural | in | Nasal | *in edhil* "the elves" |
| Genitive | en | Mixed | *en-* forms genitive phrases |

### Article Before Vowels

- Singular: **i** remains unchanged before vowels
- Plural: **in** appears in full before vowels: *in Ellath* "the Elves"
- Before consonants, the **n** of the plural article often disappears, with nasal mutation being the only trace of its presence

### Examples

- *mellon* "friend" → *i vellon* "the friend" (soft mutation)
- *barad* "tower" → *i varad* "the tower" (soft mutation b→v)
- *aran* "king" → *i aran* "the king" (no visible mutation on vowels)
- *periain* "hobbits" → *i Pheriain* "the Hobbits" (nasal mutation p→ph)

## Nouns and Plural Formation

### Overview

Sindarin nouns do not decline for case; grammatical relations are expressed through word order and prepositions. However, nouns do have plural forms created through a distinctive vowel-changing process called **i-affection** (or *prestanneth*).

### I-Affection (Umlaut)

This phenomenon is similar to Germanic languages (English "man/men," "goose/geese") and Welsh. Historically, the primitive Elvish plural ending *-i caused the preceding vowels to shift toward higher, fronter positions. The suffix later disappeared, but the vowel changes remained.

### Vowel Changes in Plurals

#### Final Syllable (and Monosyllables with Consonant Clusters)

| Singular | Plural | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| a | e | *narn* "tale" → *nern* "tales" |
| e | i | *edhel* "elf" → *edhil* "elves" |
| o | y | *orch* "orc" → *yrch* "orcs" |
| u | y | *tulus* "poplar" → *tylys* "poplars" |

#### Non-Final Syllables

| Singular | Plural | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| a | e | *adan* "man" → *edain* "men" |
| o | e | *onod* "Ent" → *enyd* "Ents" |
| u | y | *Annun* "West" → *Ennyn* "Wests" |

#### Final i-Intrusion

For monosyllables ending in a single consonant with certain vowels:

| Singular | Plural | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| a/a | ai | *bar* "home" → *bair* "homes" |
| o | ui | *thon* "pine" → *thuin* "pines" |
| u | ui | *lhug* "snake" → *lhuig* "snakes" |
| au | oe | *naug* "dwarf" → *noeg* "dwarves" |

**Diphthongs ae, oe, ei, ai, ui do not mutate.**

### Class Plural (Collective Plural)

Sindarin has a special plural form for referring to an entire category or race:

| Suffix | Usage | Example |
|--------|-------|---------|
| -ath | Default; all things of a category | *elenath* "all stars" (from *el* "star") |
| -rim | Groups of people (neutral) | *Nogothrim* "the Dwarf-folk" |
| -hoth | Groups of people (pejorative) | *orchoth* "orc-horde" |
| -waith | Peoples or regions | *Forodwaith* "Northerners" |

Examples:
- *el* "star" → *elin* (some stars) → *elenath* (all the stars)
- *Perian* "Hobbit" → *Periain* (some Hobbits) → *Periannath* (Hobbit-kind)

## Consonant Mutations

Consonant mutations are one of the most distinctive and important features of Sindarin grammar, borrowed conceptually from Welsh. The initial consonant of a word changes depending on the grammatical context.

### Types of Mutations

Sindarin has several mutation types, with soft and nasal being the most common:

1. **Soft Mutation (Lenition)** - most common
2. **Nasal Mutation**
3. **Mixed Mutation** - combination of soft and nasal
4. **Stop Mutation**
5. **Liquid Mutation** (speculative)
6. **Sibilant Mutation**

### Soft Mutation (Lenition)

The most widespread mutation, involving a "softening" of consonants:

| Original | Mutated | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| p | b | *perian* → *berian* |
| t | d | *taur* → *daur* |
| c | g | *calad* → *galad* |
| b | v | *barad* → *varad* |
| d | dh | *dagor* → *dhagor* |
| g | - (disappears) | *galadh* → *aladh* |
| m | v | *mellon* → *vellon* |
| s | h | *Sauron* → *Hauron* |
| h | ch | *herdir* → *cherdir* |
| hw | chw | *hwest* → *chwest* |
| lh | thl/l | |
| rh | thr/r | |

**Triggers for Soft Mutation:**
- Singular article *i*
- Prefixes: *athra-*, *ath-*, *go-*, *gwa-*, *u-*
- Prepositions: *ab*, *am*, *adel*, *be*, *dad*, *di*, *na*, *nu*, *i*
- After *avo* (negative imperative)
- Adjectives following nouns
- Direct objects of verbs

### Nasal Mutation

| Original | Mutated | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| p | ph | *perian* → *pherian* |
| t | th | *taur* → *thaur* |
| c | ch | *calad* → *chalad* |
| b | m | *barad* → *marad* |
| d | n | *dagor* → *nagor* |
| g | ng | *galadh* → *ngaladh* |
| m | m (unchanged) | |
| n | n (unchanged) | |

**Triggers for Nasal Mutation:**
- Plural article *in*
- Prepositions: *an*, *dan*
- Plural *'nin*

### Mixed Mutation

A combination of soft and nasal mutation effects:

| Original | Mutated |
|----------|---------|
| p | ph |
| t | th |
| c | ch |
| b | b (unchanged) |
| d | d (unchanged) |
| g | g (unchanged) |
| m | m (unchanged) |
| s | h |

**Triggers for Mixed Mutation:**
- Genitive article *en*
- Prepositions: *ben*, *erin*, *nan*, *'nin*, *uin*

### Stop Mutation

Caused by words ending historically in *-t* or *-d*:

| Original | Mutated |
|----------|---------|
| p | p (unchanged) |
| t | t (unchanged) |
| c | c (unchanged) |
| b | p |
| d | t |
| g | c |

**Triggers:** Prepositions *ed*, *ned*, *o(d)*

### Sibilant Mutation

| Original | Mutated |
|----------|---------|
| p | f |
| t | th |
| c | ch |

**Triggers:** Conjunction *ah* "and," preposition *oh*

## Adjectives

### Placement

Unlike English, Sindarin adjectives typically follow the nouns they modify:

- English: "green hills"
- Sindarin: "hills green" (*emyn gelin*)

**Exceptions:**
- Quantitative adjectives may precede: *il chem* "all hands"
- Numbers precede: *leben teil* "five feet"
- Traditional titles and names may place the adjective first

### Agreement

Adjectives must agree with their nouns in number and undergo appropriate mutations:

#### Soft Mutation After Nouns

When following a noun, adjectives undergo soft mutation:

- *beleg* "great" → *i adan veleg* "the great man"
- *maer* "good" → *i mellon vaer* "the good friend"

#### Plural Agreement

Adjectives change vowels to match plural nouns using the same i-affection rules:

- *i adan veleg* "the great man" → *in edain velig* "the great men"
- *calad* "light" → *celaid* (plural)

#### Predicative Use

In "to be" expressions with an omitted copula, adjectives do not undergo soft mutation but still agree in number:

- *i edhel beleg* "the elf [is] mighty"
- *in edhil belig* "the elves [are] mighty"

### No Gender

Sindarin has no grammatical gender distinction in nouns, adjectives, or pronouns.

## Pronouns

### Personal Pronouns

Sindarin personal pronouns function as subjects. They are often omitted because the verb itself contains person markers (pronominal suffixes).

| Person | Pronoun | Usage |
|--------|---------|-------|
| 1st singular | ni | "I" |
| 1st plural (inclusive) | gwe | "we" (includes listener) |
| 1st plural | me | "we" |
| 2nd familiar | ci | "you" (informal, singular) |
| 2nd formal singular | le | "you" (polite) |
| 2nd formal plural | de | "you" (polite, plural) |
| 3rd singular | te | "he/she/it" |
| 3rd plural | ti | "they" |

### Possessive Pronouns

Possessive pronouns follow the noun and undergo soft mutation like adjectives. The noun often takes a definite article.

| Person | Possessive | Example |
|--------|------------|---------|
| 1st singular | nin | *Adar nin* "my father" |
| 1st plural | min | *i vellyn min* "our friends" |
| 2nd formal | lin | *i thir lin* "your face" |
| 2nd informal | cin | *i vellon gin* "your friend" |
| 3rd | din/tin | *ionnath din* "his/her sons" |
| Reflexive | in | "his/her own" (refers back to subject) |

**Formation Pattern:** The possessive is formed by adding *-n* to the independent pronoun and lengthening the base vowel.

### Demonstrative Pronouns

Sindarin demonstratives are derived from the same stems as personal pronouns:

- Proximal ("this"): derived from stem S-
- Distal ("that"): derived from stem TA-

## Verbs

Sindarin verbs are highly inflected, marking tense, number, and person through suffixes and stem modifications.

### Verb Classes

1. **Basic Verbs (I-stem):** Root verbs without suffixes, often shown with consonant endings: *ped-* "to say"
2. **Derived Verbs (A-stem):** Verbs with stems ending in -a: *teitha-* "to write"

### Moods

Sindarin has three moods:

| Mood | Usage | Formation |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Indicative | Statements of fact | Various tense forms |
| Imperative | Commands | Ending *-o* |
| Subjunctive/Optative | Wishes, possibilities | *aen* + future tense |

### Tenses

#### Present Tense

Formed from the ancient Common Eldarin aorist. Covers both simple present ("I eat") and present progressive ("I am eating").

For basic verbs, the present typically shows the stem with appropriate person suffixes:
- *ped-* "speak" → *pedin* "I speak"

#### Past Tense

Two formation patterns:

1. **Weak (suffix-based):** Adding endings to the stem
2. **Strong (stem-changing):** Internal modification, often nasal infixion

Examples:
- *ped-* → *pent* "spoke" (nasal infixion)
- *teitha-* → *teithant* "drew" (suffix)

#### Future Tense

Formed by adding *-atha* to the stem:
- *nor-* "run" → *noratha* "will run"
- *linna-* "sing" → *linnatha* "will sing"

### Person Markers (Pronominal Suffixes)

Sindarin verbs take pronominal suffixes to indicate the subject:

| Person | Suffix | Example |
|--------|--------|---------|
| 1st singular | -n | *linnon* "I sing" |
| 1st plural | -m | *linnam* "we sing" |
| 2nd singular | -ch (?) | limited attestation |
| 2nd plural | -l (?) | reconstructed |
| 3rd singular | (none) | *linna* "sings" |
| 3rd plural | -r | *linnar* "they sing" |

**Note:** The 3rd singular has no pronominal suffix; the 3rd plural uses *-r* which also functions for subject-verb agreement.

### Imperative

The imperative is formed with the ending *-o* and covers all persons:

- *daro!* "halt!"
- *tolo!* "come!"
- *pedo!* "speak!"
- *lacho calad! Drego morn!* "Flame light! Flee night!"

**Negative Imperative:** Use *avo* before the verb:
- *avo garo!* "don't do it!"
- Sometimes as prefix: *avgaro!*

### Invariable Forms

#### Infinitive/Gerund

Sindarin may not distinguish between infinitives and gerunds. The verbal noun serves both functions:

- Basic verbs: stem + *-ed* (*cared* "doing")
- Derived verbs: stem (minus -a) + *-d* or *-o*

#### Participles

**Active (Present) Participle:**
- Basic verbs: *-el* (*carel* "doing")
- Derived verbs: *-ol* (*teitho* → *teithol* "writing")

**Perfective (Past) Active Participle:**
- Ending *-iel* with vowel lengthening: *cariel* "having done"

**Passive Participle:**
- Basic verbs: past stem + *-nen*: *tirnen* "guarded"
- Derived verbs: stem + *-nnen*: *orthannen* "raised"

## Prepositions

Sindarin uses prepositions (not postpositions) which trigger various mutations on the following noun.

### Common Prepositions and Their Mutations

#### Soft Mutation (Lenition)

| Preposition | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| ab | after, following |
| adel | behind, in rear of |
| am | up, upon |
| be | according to |
| dad | down |
| di | beneath |
| na | to, toward, at |
| nu | under |
| i | before |

#### Nasal Mutation

| Preposition | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| an | to, for |
| dan | against, back |

#### Mixed Mutation

| Preposition | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| ben | according to |
| erin | on the |
| nan | with the, to the |
| 'nin | for the |
| uin | from the |

#### Stop Mutation

| Preposition | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| ed | out of |
| ned | in |
| o(d) | from, of |

#### Liquid Mutation (Speculative)

| Preposition | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| or | over, above |

#### Sibilant Mutation

| Preposition | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| oh | about, concerning |

## Sentence Structure

### Basic Word Order

Sindarin primarily uses **Subject-Verb-Object (SVO)** order, similar to English but different from Welsh (which is VSO):

- *Adan nor* "A man runs" (subject + verb)
- *Cenin 'aladh* "I see a tree" (verb + object with mutation)

### Head-Initial Structure

Sindarin is consistently head-initial:
- Verbs precede objects
- Articles precede nouns
- Prepositions precede their objects

### Object Placement and Mutation

**Direct objects** follow the verb and undergo soft mutation:
- *Cenin **'aladh*** "I see **a tree**" (galadh → 'aladh)

**Indirect objects** follow direct objects (opposite of English):
- *Onen i-Estel Edain* "I gave Hope to the Edain"

### Pronoun Objects

Pronoun objects may precede the verb, especially in poetic or formal contexts:
- *le linnathon* "to thee I will sing" (from "A Elbereth Gilthoniel")

### Copula Omission

The verb "to be" is often omitted in equative sentences:
- *i edhel beleg* "the elf [is] mighty"

### Genitive Construction

The genitive is expressed by placing the modifying noun after the modified noun (adjectival position):
- *aran Moria* "king of Moria"
- *ennyn Durin* "doors of Durin"

## Numbers

Sindarin uses a decimal (base-10) counting system, though the Elves historically preferred counting in sixes and twelves for time-keeping.

### Cardinal Numbers 1-12

| Number | Sindarin | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| 1 | min | |
| 2 | tad | |
| 3 | nel, neledh | |
| 4 | canad | |
| 5 | leben | |
| 6 | eneg | |
| 7 | odog | |
| 8 | toloth | |
| 9 | neder | |
| 10 | pae (earlier: cae) | -paen in compounds |
| 11 | minig | |
| 12 | uiug | |

### Higher Numbers

| Number | Sindarin |
|--------|----------|
| 20 | tatphaen |
| 30 | nelphaen |
| 100 | (various forms attested) |
| 1,000 | meneg (pl. menig) |

### Compound Numbers

Numbers 13-19, 21-29, etc. are formed using "and" (*a*):

- 33: *Nelphaen a neledh* "thirty and three"
- 67: *Enephaen a odog* "sixty and seven"

### Number Placement

Numbers precede the nouns they modify:
- *leben teil* "five feet"
- *Lebennin* "Five Rivers"
- *Ossiriand* "Land of Seven Rivers"

### Examples in Names

- *Argonath* "The Two Kings" (pair of royal stones)
- *Menegroth* "The Thousand Caves"

## Conjunctions and Relative Clauses

### Coordinating Conjunctions

#### "And"

| Form | Usage |
|------|-------|
| a | Before consonants |
| ah | Before vowels |

The conjunction may trigger sibilant mutation, though this is considered optional in Neo-Sindarin.

Example: *Athrabeth Finrod ah Andreth* "The Debate of Finrod and Andreth"

#### "Or"

*egor* - "or"

### Relative Pronoun

The indeclinable relative pronoun **i** introduces relative clauses, functioning like English "who," "which," or "that":

- Used for both people and things (unlike English)
- Used for both restrictive and non-restrictive clauses
- Cannot be modified or declined

Example: *i edhel i linna* "the elf who sings"
